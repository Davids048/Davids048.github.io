Warmer World, More Disasters?

Climate disasters since 1980, from the IMF Climate Change Indicators Dashboard. The world warmed by about 1 °C, and recorded disasters went up. Did the heat cause the rise? Before you look at the data, make a guess.

1. Draw your guess

The chart starts at 104 recorded climate disasters in 1980. Drag across the chart to draw how you think the yearly count changed until 2022, then press Reveal.

2. The world got warmer…

Each bar is one year of world surface temperature change relative to the 1951–1980 average. Since 1980, almost every year has been warmer than that baseline, and 2022 was 1.4 °C warmer. Hover a bar to see the value.

3. …and recorded disasters rose, but only until about 2000

Recorded disasters rose 2.6× from the 1980s to the 2000s, then leveled off while the warming went on. Floods and storms make up about 80% of the total. Click a type in the legend to show or hide it; hover to read a year.

4. Part of the rise is better reporting

In 1980 only 61 countries had any recorded climate disaster; after 2000 about 113 countries did each year. Switch the view to see the count per reporting country, which grows 1.4× from the 1980s to the 2000s instead of 2.6×.

5. Warmer years, more disasters?

One dot per year. Drag on the timeline below the scatter plot to pick a range of years. The selected years are highlighted, and the correlation and fit line update. Try 1980–1999, then 2000–2022.

6. Test the claim for one place

Pick a disaster type. The ranking shows the countries whose yearly count rose most from 1980–1999 to 2000–2022. Click a bar, or pick a country in the menu, to see its disasters and its warming year by year.

Prototype write-up

What have you done so far?

I joined two IMF Climate Change Indicators tables, the EM-DAT climate-related disaster counts and the FAO surface temperature change, into one JSON file with a world series and a per-country series for 1980–2022. I laid out the full structure of the explorable explanation, from the opening guess to the country explorer, and wrote short descriptive text for each step. Every section already has a working D3 chart: a draw-your-guess hook, the warming bars, a stacked area chart with type toggles, a reporting-coverage chart with three views, a year-brushed scatter plot, and a linked ranking and country chart. Tooltips, the legend toggles, the brush, and the country and type pickers all work, but the styling, annotations and transitions are still rough. The world map that I planned in the proposal is not built yet; the ranking and the country menu stand in for it now.

What will be the most challenging of your project to design and why?

The hardest part will be to show the reporting caveat without hiding the real warming signal, because the same rise in counts mixes real change and better record keeping. If I stress the caveat too much, readers may leave thinking that climate change has no effect on disasters, which the data also does not show. A second challenge is the world map: small island states have many disasters per person but are almost invisible on a map, while large countries such as China and the United States dominate any raw count. I will need to choose between raw counts, counts per reporting year and rises over time, and make that choice clear in the legend. Finally, the scroll story must link the charts so that a brush or a country pick in one view carries over to the next views without confusing the reader.

Data: IMF Climate Change Indicators Dashboard (climatedata.imf.org): EM-DAT, CRED / UCLouvain climate-related disaster frequency; FAO surface temperature change. Counts are recorded disasters, so they depend on reporting. Source code: GitHub.
